## 4.1 Summary of main findings

In a four-wave EMA study of 391 first-year undergraduates, momentary stress drove momentary suicidal ideation predominantly through entrapment, with smaller but credible contributions from perceived burdensomeness and thwarted belongingness; the cascade was moderated path- and dimension-specifically by childhood trauma, attenuated on the dominant *b*-path by trait protective resources, and varied credibly in slope strength across individuals. The signature finding, however, was structural rather than quantitative: three data-driven dynamic SI subtypes dissociated from the static risk–resource trait gradient, with two subtypes (C2 and C3) credibly elevated above the low-stable subtype (C1) on the within-person cascade but not credibly different from each other on the two largest indirect pathways, despite being strictly graded on every person-level risk and resource indicator we measured.

## 4.2 Dynamic phenotypes dissociate from static trait severity

The dissociation between the indirect-cascade gradient and the trait-severity gradient is best read as a construct distinction rather than as a failure of monotonicity. The three subtypes were strictly graded C1 < C2 < C3 on every risk indicator examined and inversely graded on every resource indicator; on the same individuals, however, the within-person stress-to-SI cascade saturated short of maximal trait severity, with C2 and C3 not credibly different on the entrapment- or burdensomeness-mediated indirect pathways. A complementary observation reinforces the same point: the raw stress-to-SI slope was credibly steeper in C3 than in C2, yet this residual slope difference was fully absorbed once the three concurrent proximal mediators were partialled out. What separates the most severely impaired students from the high-fluctuating but trait-milder ones, in other words, appears to be trait severity itself — not the moment-to-moment cognitive route through which stress reaches ideation.

This pattern is consistent with a growing literature treating short-term affective dynamics — emotional inertia, intra-individual variability, and stress reactivity — as risk markers that index a phenotype distinct from, rather than reducible to, mean symptom level (Houben et al., 2015; Kuppens et al., 2010). On that reading, the C2 subtype — relatively mild on trait severity but high on within-person fluctuation — is not simply a less severe version of C3, but a structurally different risk profile in which the *coupling* between stress and cognitive appraisal is steep even where the *level* of impairment is moderate. The dissociation therefore asks the field to keep two answers to the question "who is at risk?" in parallel: one calibrated to where individuals sit on a static severity gradient, and one calibrated to how reactively their internal states respond to daily perturbations.

We are explicit about what this finding does and does not claim. It does not assert C2-versus-C3 equivalence — the region-of-practical-equivalence analysis ruled out equivalence as well as difference, placing the C3-versus-C2 contrast in a high-uncertainty zone where neither directional difference nor practical similarity can be defended on these data (Kruschke, 2018). Nor does the finding overturn the well-replicated trait-severity gradient — Hypothesis 7 was supported on every indicator. The claim is narrower and more useful: two reasonable operationalisations of "high-risk" — graded trait profile and steep moment-to-moment indirect cascade — pick out partially separable populations, and conflating them risks both over-treating C3 (by assuming maximal trait severity implies maximal moment-level reactivity) and under-detecting C2 (whose trait scores will not trip standard screens).

Prior EMA work has documented within-person SI fluctuation (Hallensleben et al., 2019; Kleiman et al., 2017) and identified moment-level proximal risk factors among high-risk samples (Czyz et al., 2019), but few studies have asked whether data-driven dynamic phenotypes recapitulate or diverge from the static trait gradient on which the same individuals can be ranked. The present results add to this small but consequential literature by suggesting that within-person cascade reactivity saturates short of maximal trait severity, and that the dynamic-phenotype dimension is informative beyond what trait-severity gradients already index.

## 4.3 Path- and dimension-specific moderation by trauma and resources

The pattern of childhood-trauma moderation refines stress-sensitisation theory in two complementary ways. First, it is path-selective: trauma moderation concentrated on the *b*-path linking momentary mediators to SI rather than on the stress-to-mediator *a*-path, consistent with childhood adversity shaping the translation of cognitive-appraisal states into self-injurious thinking more than the perception of stressors themselves (Compas et al., 2017; McLaughlin et al., 2020). Second, it is dimension-selective: emotional abuse broadly amplified mediator-to-SI coupling across all four mediators, sexual abuse selectively amplified the cognitive-mediator pathways (entrapment and burdensomeness), and the neglect dimensions selectively amplified the relational pathways (thwarted belongingness and loneliness) — a pattern in which the form of childhood maltreatment leaves a fingerprint on the proximal channel through which adult stress reaches ideation (Liu et al., 2017). Trait resilience, social connectedness, and habitual cognitive reappraisal each moderated the dominant entrapment-to-SI *b*-path in the protective direction, while personality dysfunction moderated the identical pathway in the amplifying direction. The dual-axis specification therefore operates as predicted on the strongest proximal channel: risk and resource act on the same *b*-path with opposite signs (Masten, 2018).

One unexpected pattern was the reverse-direction moderation of physical abuse on the stress-to-entrapment and stress-to-burdensomeness *a*-paths, with emotional abuse amplifying these couplings and physical abuse attenuating them. This may reflect trauma-related stress-decoupling, in which severe physical victimisation in childhood blunts rather than amplifies the moment-to-moment translation of everyday stressors into appraisal-based cognitions — a downstream signature consistent with the dissociative phenotype identified in the broader trauma literature (Lanius et al., 2010). Because the pattern is small, was not pre-registered, and emerged in a non-PA-enriched sample, this observation is exploratory and warrants replication in samples with adequate variance on physical-abuse exposure before any mechanistic claim is sharpened.

## 4.4 Methodological considerations

Estimating three concurrent within-person mediators in a single Bayesian multivariate multilevel model with cross-equation random-effect covariance allowed the question of mediator dominance to be answered without sequential model-selection bias and with explicit propagation of uncertainty through every parameter, including the indirect-effect contrasts on which the dissociation rests (Bürkner, 2017; McNeish & Hamaker, 2020). The pre-registered frequentist pre-analysis converged on the same qualitative pattern as the Bayesian main model, providing method-independent validation that the dissociation is not an artefact of the prior structure. We note that the cascade as estimated here is contemporaneous: within-prompt co-occurrence does not establish causal direction, and the contemporaneous specification is a feature of the design — appropriate to the sub-hourly time-scale on which appraisal and ideation co-vary — rather than a claim about temporal precedence.

## 4.5 Limitations

Several limitations qualify these conclusions. First, the C3 subsample is small (37 participants, 79 person-waves), which limits statistical power for the C3-versus-C2 contrast; because the central claim is dissociation — a non-credibly-different finding supported by ROPE-confirmed undecidedness rather than by a positive equivalence test — insufficient power renders the claim conservative rather than inflated, but confirmation in a larger high-severity sample remains an important next step. Second, momentary SI was measured by a single item, consistent with the EMA-SI literature on which this design is built (Hallensleben et al., 2019; Kleiman et al., 2017) but limited in scope; multi-item momentary assessment may recover additional variance and disambiguate passive from active ideation. Third, all within-person variables were self-reported at the same prompt, and although temporal autocorrelation was addressed via an AR(1)-controlled sensitivity model, contemporaneous common-method variance cannot be ruled out. Fourth, the sample is a single-university Chinese first-year cohort with a particular ethnic and developmental profile; generalisation to community, clinical, or non-Han samples awaits replication. Fifth, differential EMA compliance across subtypes was tested in a 20-minute-latency subsample and the dissociation pattern was robust, but compliance-driven selection effects in larger high-severity samples may yet differ.

## 4.6 Clinical implications

Three intervention-relevant implications follow. First, the C2 profile — relatively normal trait scores but a steep within-person cascade — is likely invisible to trait-only screening, suggesting that EMA-style ambulatory monitoring should be considered a complement to, not a substitute for, conventional risk assessment among first-year undergraduates. Second, the C3 profile is already detectable by standard trait screening but does not appear to require the most intensive EMA dose, since its dynamic cascade does not credibly exceed C2's; resources may therefore be better allocated to identifying volatile-but-trait-mild students than to further intensifying monitoring of those the trait screen already flags. Third, because entrapment carries the dominant within-person indirect effect, micro-interventions that target perceived blocked-escape cognitions — for example, acceptance- and reappraisal-based defusion delivered in just-in-time adaptive formats (Nahum-Shani et al., 2018) — are the highest-yield candidates for moment-level intervention, complemented by resilience-, connectedness-, and reappraisal-training as *b*-path levers and by trauma-informed care as an *a*-path lever for survivors of emotional abuse and neglect.

## 4.7 Conclusion

The opening question of this paper was, by what moment-to-moment mechanism does stress convert into suicidal ideation, and whose cascade is steepest. Our findings admit a two-part answer. The cascade runs primarily through entrapment, jointly tuned by childhood-trauma exposure on the *a*-path and by trait resources on the *b*-path. And the cascade is steepest not in the most severely impaired students, but in those whose trajectories are most volatile — with the high-fluctuating and high-severity subtypes dynamically indistinguishable on the two largest indirect pathways despite being strictly separable on every static trait profile we measured. Identifying who needs which intervention, on this evidence, will require both: trait screening and within-person monitoring are complementary windows on a single phenomenon.
