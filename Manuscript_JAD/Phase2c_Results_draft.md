## 3.1 Sample characteristics and EMA descriptives

The analytic sample comprised 391 first-year undergraduates (1,553 person-waves; mean age 20.4 years, SD 1.6, range 17–25; 67.3% female; 92.6% Han) who contributed 28,113 valid ecological momentary assessment (EMA) prompts across the four-wave protocol (three prompts per day for seven days per wave). The overall prompt-level response rate was 76.4% (SD 11.2%). Approximately 46.3% of prompts captured at least one concurrent stressor, and 6.9% captured momentary suicidal ideation (SI) above the floor of the single-item scale. Descriptive statistics for all EMA constructs, overall and by subtype and wave, are provided in Table 1 and Table S1.

## 3.2 Within-person mediation, mediator dominance, and slope variance (H1–H3)

Hypothesis 1 proposed that momentary stress would predict momentary SI within persons through three concurrent proximal pathways. All three indirect effects were credible: the within-person stress→entrapment→SI pathway returned a posterior median of 0.0048 (95% credible interval [CrI] [0.0036, 0.0061]; PD > 0.999); stress→perceived burdensomeness→SI returned 0.0023 (95% CrI [0.0014, 0.0033]; PD > 0.999); and stress→thwarted belongingness→SI returned 0.0012 (95% CrI [0.0006, 0.0019]; PD > 0.999) (Table 2, Fig. 2a). H1 was fully supported. Approximately 55% of the total within-person stress-to-SI cascade was carried by the entrapment pathway alone.

Hypothesis 2 proposed that the entrapment-mediated indirect effect would exceed the two interpersonal pathways. The pairwise posterior probability that the entrapment indirect effect exceeded the burdensomeness indirect effect was above 0.99, as was the corresponding probability against the belongingness pathway (Table 2, Fig. 2b). H2 was fully supported: entrapment carried the dominant within-person indirect pathway.

Hypothesis 3 addressed inter-individual heterogeneity in cascade slopes. Across the three mediators, the within-person random-slope standard deviations ranged from 0.043 to 0.056 on the *a*-paths (stress→mediator) and from 0.073 to 0.124 on the *b*-paths (mediator→SI); all six 95% CrIs excluded zero (Table S7). H3 was fully supported: individuals differed credibly from one another in the strength of both halves of the cascade, indicating that the average cascade obscures meaningful person-level variation that is recovered in the moderation analyses that follow.

## 3.3 Path- and dimension-specific moderation by childhood trauma (H4)

Hypothesis 4 addressed whether childhood trauma — measured by the five Childhood Trauma Questionnaire (CTQ) subscales for emotional abuse (EA), physical abuse (PA), sexual abuse (SA), emotional neglect (EN), and physical neglect (PN) — moderates the cascade selectively by path and by dimension.

*Mediator-to-SI b-paths.* Of the 20 *b*-path interactions (five CTQ subscales × four mediators), at least four exceeded the pre-registered threshold of PD ≥ 0.975 (Table 3, Fig. 3a). Hypothesis 4a was supported: CTQ moderation concentrated on the *b*-path. The pattern was dimension-specific. EA produced credible positive moderation across all four *b*-paths, identifying it as the broadest amplifier of mediator-to-SI coupling. PA, in contrast, produced near-null moderation across all *b*-paths; this confirmed Hypothesis 4c — PA exhibits the weakest *b*-path moderation of the five CTQ dimensions. SA produced selective positive moderation on the entrapment→SI and burdensomeness→SI *b*-paths. The neglect dimensions targeted relational mediators specifically: EN produced credible positive moderation on the belongingness→SI and loneliness→SI *b*-paths, with PN displaying a similar but weaker pattern on the same relational pathways.

*Stress-to-mediator a-paths.* Although the pre-registered primary expectation was *b*-path concentration, the *a*-path interactions revealed an informative complementary pattern (Fig. 3b). EA produced credible positive interactions on all four *a*-paths, amplifying the stress→mediator coupling broadly. On the *a*-path, PA showed reverse-direction moderation compared to EA on stress→entrapment and stress→burden. The neglect dimensions produced credible positive moderation on stress→belongingness and stress→loneliness, consistent with the relational-specificity pattern observed on the *b*-paths. The full 40-model coefficient matrix for the *a*-path × CTQ-dimension and *b*-path × CTQ-dimension interactions is provided in Table S5; interpretation of the PA reverse-direction finding is addressed in Section 4.

## 3.4 Trait-resource moderation of the dominant b-path (H5)

Hypothesis 5 proposed that trait protective resources — resilience, social connectedness, and habitual cognitive reappraisal — would moderate the entrapment-to-SI *b*-path in the protective direction. All three resource indicators produced credible negative interactions with momentary entrapment on the SI outcome: the Brief Resilience Scale (BRS), the connectedness composite, and the cognitive reappraisal subscale of the Emotion Regulation Questionnaire (ERQ) each yielded posterior medians with 95% CrIs that excluded zero on the negative side (Table S6, Fig. 4). Higher trait resilience, higher connectedness, and higher habitual cognitive reappraisal each attenuated the within-person slope from momentary entrapment to momentary SI. H5 was fully supported.

As a complement, the Level of Personality Functioning Scale–Brief Form (LPFS-BF) was entered as a risk control on the same *b*-path. Its interaction direction was positive, indicating amplification — the opposite sign to the three resource indicators on the identical pathway. The resource indicators (negative interaction) and the LPFS-BF risk control (positive interaction) thus produced opposing-sign moderation on the same proximal channel. The substantive implications for path-specific intervention design are taken up in Section 4.

## 3.5 Dynamic subtypes, the static trait gradient, and their dissociation (H6–H7)

Three dynamic subtypes of within-person SI were identified by latent profile analysis at the person-wave level; because the same individual could occupy different subtypes across the four EMA waves, the participant counts below sum to more than 391 (Table 4, footnote; see Methods §2.4). The three subtypes were a low-stable subtype (C1; 367 participants, 1,275 person-waves [82.1%], 23,104 prompts), a low-to-moderate, high-fluctuating subtype (C2; 128 participants, 199 person-waves [12.8%], 3,637 prompts), and a high-intensity, high-fluctuating subtype (C3; 37 participants, 79 person-waves [5.1%], 1,372 prompts).

*H6 — Subtype-specific indirect-effect gradient.* Hypothesis 6 predicted a strictly monotonic gradient C1 < C2 < C3 in the entrapment-mediated within-person indirect effect, with a pre-registered monotonicity threshold of PD ≥ 0.90. The subtype-specific indirect-effect posteriors are summarized in Table 4 and Fig. 5. C2 > C1 and C3 > C1 contrasts were credible across all three mediators (95% CrIs excluded zero). However, C3 > C2 strict monotonicity was not supported for entrapment (PD < 0.90) or burdensomeness (PD < 0.90, with burdensomeness showing a numerically reversed point estimate). For belongingness, the C3 > C2 contrast was credible (PD ≥ 0.975). Taken together, the indirect effect gradient was not strictly monotonic: C2 and C3 were not credibly different on the two largest indirect pathways. H6 was therefore partially supported.

*H7 — Static risk–resource trait gradient.* Hypothesis 7 predicted strict monotonic differentiation of the three subtypes on every person-level risk and resource indicator. The pattern was confirmed for every indicator examined (Fig. 1). On the risk side, CTQ emotional abuse, CTQ emotional neglect, and the LPFS-BF total each increased strictly monotonically across C1 → C2 → C3. On the resource side, the BRS and the connectedness composite each decreased strictly monotonically across the same gradient. Every trait-level contrast was in the predicted direction, and H7 was fully supported.

*The dissociation.* Thus, the dynamic cascade reactivity and the static trait severity index are not redundant. The three subtypes were strictly graded on every person-level risk and resource indicator (H7), yet the within-person indirect effect gradient was not — C2 and C3, despite their distinct trait profiles, were dynamically indistinguishable on the two largest indirect pathways. We return to the substantive implications of this dynamic-static dissociation, and to the C2-versus-C3 framing of the within-person cascade, in Section 4.3.
